The Blind Spot Index (working title)

Solar panels on homes and businesses generate electricity the grid operator never directly measures — it only sees net demand, true consumption minus whatever solar silently offset. As that hidden capacity grows, a stretch of sun followed by clouds stops looking like weather and starts looking like demand appearing from nowhere. This pipeline builds a physics-based index of that blind spot, then validates it statistically against real observed demand, region by region — currently CAISO (PG&E, SCE, SDG&E), 2025.

The core idea

Two layers, computed independently. Layer 1 is a physics-only index — installed solar capacity × weather/irradiance, sized against a region's peak demand — computable anywhere with capacity and weather data, even without good demand data. Layer 2 validates it: a regression of real net load against an irradiance anomaly, controlling for temperature (via cooling/heating degree-hours, not a plain linear term — cloudy days are often cooler days, and a naive control would misattribute some of temperature's effect to solar) and calendar effects. The regression's irradiance coefficient — the sensitivity rate — is a real, observed measurement that independently confirms the physics-only index isn't just a guess.

Ingest (CAISO OASIS demand + NSRDB weather + Tracking the Sun capacity) → Silver (fact/dim tables, DuckDB locally / Fabric Lakehouse) → Gold (Blind Spot Index + sensitivity-rate regression) → Serve (Power BI Direct Lake report + public site)

Three inputs, none sufficient alone

  • Installed capacity — how much hidden generation exists, by utility territory (Berkeley Lab / NREL Tracking the Sun).
  • Weather / irradiance — how much of that capacity is actually producing at a given hour (NREL NSRDB, satellite-derived).
  • Observed net load — the only thing the grid operator actually measures (CAISO OASIS, hourly, per TAC zone).

Notes

  • Temperature is controlled for explicitly (CDD/HDD), not folded into the irradiance signal.
  • CAISO's three utility territories (PG&E, SCE, SDG&E) are validated independently rather than blended into one statewide number.
  • The local DuckDB build and the Fabric/PySpark port are cross-validated against each other — same index values, same worst-swing hours, to floating-point noise.
  • Ambiguous capacity-data edge cases (a handful of small municipal utilities) are tagged with a confidence flag rather than silently included or dropped.
