What am I looking at?

Solar panels on homes and businesses ("behind-the-meter" or "rooftop" solar) generate electricity the grid operator never directly measures. It only sees the net demand left over after that generation offsets it. When clouds roll in and rooftop solar output drops, that hidden generation disappears and demand can jump, with no warning, because maybe the grid operator was never watching it in the first place. That's the blind spot this site measures.

The numbers below come in two layers: a physics-based estimate (installed solar capacity × weather, no real demand data required) and a statistical validation against real, observed demand, so the physics-only estimate isn't just a guess. Data covers California's CAISO grid, full year 2025.

Regions

PG&E
Pacific Gas & Electric: serves Northern and Central California.
SCE
Southern California Edison: serves most of Southern California, excluding San Diego.
SDG&E
San Diego Gas & Electric: serves San Diego and southern Orange County.

Together these three utility territories make up CAISO, the operator of California's electric grid. Demand is tracked separately for each because CAISO publishes real hourly demand at this grain, not just as one statewide number.

Metrics

Blind Spot Index
The share of a region's peak demand that could appear suddenly, with no warning, if the sun goes behind a cloud, computed from installed solar capacity and weather physics alone, sized against that region's peak demand.
Sensitivity rate
The real change in demand from hour to hour, based on how sunny or cloudy it is, isolated from other causes like temperature and holidays.
Total installed capacity
Total behind-the-meter solar capacity installed in the region, in MW, as of the most recent data available (covers residential and commercial/industrial rooftop systems).
Estimated BTM generation
How much electricity behind-the-meter solar is estimated to be producing at each hour of the year. This is the hidden power generation the grid operator never directly measures, and the Blind Spot Index is drawn straight from its worst hour-to-hour swing.
